GUARD

When Can You Trust the Certificate?

Split-conformal calibration turns any AI-text detector score into an accusation rule with a finite-sample bound on the false-accusation rate. GUARD maps empirically where that certificate holds, where it breaks, and how a deployer can detect the breakage before anyone is accused.

Summary

The certificate asymmetry

The split-conformal false-accusation bound (flag a text as AI only when its conformal p-value ≤ α) depends only on the exchangeability of human calibration and human test text — the AI distribution is irrelevant to validity. That asymmetry yields sharp, testable predictions: the certificate must survive generator shift and AI evasion attacks untouched, while human-side changes (population shift, human edits, calibration leakage, length heterogeneity) can silently break it. GUARD tests all of these conditions with five scorers (GPT-2 log-likelihood, log-rank, entropy, Fast-DetectGPT, and a supervised TF-IDF model) on MAiDE-up hotel reviews and RAID pools, 200 calibration/test repeats per cell.

Key findings

  • The certificate holds in-domain, exactly as theory says. At α = 0.05 the empirical FPR is 0.049–0.051 for all five scorers, and the full FPR distribution matches the Beta envelope.
  • Human population shift breaks it — by design, and detectably. Calibrated on hotel-review humans, tested on other review-platform humans: FPR up to 0.526 at α = 0.05 (entropy), a 10× violation. On scientific-abstract humans: up to 0.247.
  • Human-edit attacks did not inflate FPR here. Function-word and synonym edits on human text pushed scores in the conservative direction (FPR ≤ 0.005 at 30% edit rate for GPT-2 scorers); an LLM-style cleanup proxy left FPR at the nominal level.
  • Power is the price, and evasion attacks pay it. Certified at α = 0.05, clean TPR ranges from 0.193 (entropy) to 0.879 (TF-IDF); a 30% synonym evasion attack collapses GPT-2-statistic scorers to ≤ 0.020 TPR while TF-IDF retains 0.834.
  • Length heterogeneity hides per-bin violations; Mondrian fixes them. Marginal calibration yields per-length-bin FPR up to 0.183 at α = 0.05 (log-likelihood, longest reviews); per-bin Mondrian calibration brings every bin to 0.030–0.046.

Method

The certificate, in one picture

Split-conformal certification: score a test text, rank it against 250 held-out human calibration scores to get a conformal p-value, and flag it as AI only when p ≤ α. The resulting finite-sample guarantee FPR ≤ α depends only on human-side exchangeability — which is exactly where GUARD goes looking for failures.

Scope

Honest limitations

What this work does not claim

  • No new conformal method. The certificate is standard split conformal prediction. The contribution is the empirical validity map, the deployment protocols (entity-grouped and Mondrian calibration, pilot diagnostics), and the certificate-asymmetry framing.
  • The certificate breaks under human population shift — by design. The bound assumes exchangeable humans; GUARD's point is that this assumption fails measurably (FPR up to 0.526 at α = 0.05) and that the failure is detectable with a small pilot sample of in-population human text before deployment. A deployer who skips the pilot inherits the violation.
  • No SOTA power claims. Scorers are deliberately modest (one GPT-2 forward pass, or TF-IDF); at α = 0.05 clean TPR is as low as 0.193, and synonym evasion collapses the GPT-2-statistic scorers almost entirely.
  • Human-edit results are proxy-based and scorer-dependent. Lexical edits (function-word, synonym, cleanup) happened to be conservative for these scorers; that is an empirical observation, not a guarantee for paraphrase-model edits, which are left to the released protocol at full scale.
  • Scope: English reviews and abstracts, eight RAID generators plus GPT-4 hotel reviews; single calibration size (n = 250) per cell.
